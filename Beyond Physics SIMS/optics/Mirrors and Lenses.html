5E Formative Assessment

Paraxial Geometric Optics

Answer ten questions using the simulation, Gaussian image equation, magnification relation, and ray-tracing model. Correct responses are not revealed in this student view.

1 / 10
Engage Question 01 / 10

Using the default object distance of 30.0 cm, state where a plane mirror forms the image. Describe its distance, orientation, and nature, and use the simulation’s sign convention for dᵢ.

Engage Question 02 / 10

Set a concave mirror to the default dₒ = 30.0 cm and |f| = 20.0 cm. The object is beyond C. What type of image forms? Support the observation with a ray diagram description.

Explore Question 03 / 10

For a concave mirror with f = +10 cm and dₒ = 30 cm, calculate the signed image distance dᵢ. Show the Gaussian equation and identify the image side.

Explore Question 04 / 10

Predict the image produced by a convex mirror for dₒ = 20.0 cm and f = −20.0 cm. Verify your prediction with dᵢ and m.

Explain Question 05 / 10

Why can a convex lens form a real, inverted image? Cite the parallel-ray refraction rule and connect it to the Gaussian image equation.

Explain Question 06 / 10

State the sign convention for a virtual image distance. Explain why a negative dᵢ does not mean the same physical side for a lens and a mirror.

Elaborate Question 07 / 10

Move the object to the focal point of either a convex lens or a concave mirror. Describe the outgoing or reflected rays and the limiting image location as dₒ approaches f.

Elaborate Question 08 / 10

Compare a concave mirror and a convex lens, each with f > 0. For dₒ > f, dₒ < f, and dₒ = f, state where each real or virtual image forms.

Evaluate Question 09 / 10

AP FRQ-style calculation: A convex lens has f = +15 cm and an upright object has dₒ = 10 cm and hₒ = 4 cm. (a) Calculate dᵢ. (b) Calculate m and hᵢ. (c) Classify the image as real or virtual, upright or inverted, and enlarged or diminished.

Evaluate Question 10 / 10

AP FRQ-style ray diagram: Sketch the image of an upright object in front of a convex mirror. Draw at least two reflected rays and their dashed back-traces. Justify the image’s virtual, upright, and diminished character.
