5E Formative Assessment: Wave Mechanics

Transverse, Longitudinal & EM Waves Exploration

1. ENGAGE
1. ENGAGE (Q1)
Open the simulation and watch the "Transverse (String)" wave and the "Longitudinal (Sound Air / Spring)" wave. How do the movements of the string and the blue gas particles look different?
1. ENGAGE (Q2)
Choose the "Electromagnetic (Light Field)" wave. Read the pink box on the screen; does a light wave need a physical material (like a string or mass) to travel?
2. EXPLORE (Q3)
Move the "Amplitude (A)" slider left and right. What happens to the size of the wave when you make the amplitude bigger?
2. EXPLORE (Q4)
Turn on the "Show Ruler" box and move the "Frequency (f)" slider to change the number. Use the ruler to measure one full wave (λ); does the wave get longer or shorter when the frequency goes up?
3. EXPLAIN (Q5)
A "transverse" wave moves up and down, but a "longitudinal" wave moves left and right. Look at the text for the longitudinal wave; what do the words "Compression" and "Rarefaction" mean for how close the blue gas particles are to each other?
3. EXPLAIN (Q6)
Choose the "Electromagnetic" wave. Look at how the blue line (E) and the pink line (B) move together to make the wave. Do they cross each other at a right angle, and what does the stats box say about how fast they travel?
4. ELABORATE (Q7)
Under "Boundary Condition," select "Fixed End 🔒" and watch the wave bounce back. Now select "Free End ⭕". How does the returning wave look different between these two choices?
4. ELABORATE (Q8)
Under "Driver Engine," change the setting from "Continuous Oscillator" to "Manual Pulse Generator". Click the "⚡ Fire Pulse" button; what is the difference between a single pulse and a continuous wave?
5. EVALUATE (Q9)
Waves carry energy from one place to another. Look at the "Energy Distribution Bar" at the bottom of the screen. What two types of energy (KE and PE) make up the total energy of the wave?
5. EVALUATE (Q10)
Think about the three waves you saw: Transverse, Longitudinal, and Electromagnetic. Write one simple, short sentence for each wave to explain how it looks when it moves.
