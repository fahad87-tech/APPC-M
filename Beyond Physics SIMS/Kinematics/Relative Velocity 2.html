Engage Question 1 of 10

Engage Question 2 of 10
Set Car 2 to travel West and Car 1 to travel North. With Car 1 as the observer, in what direction does the relative velocity vector point? Explain in terms of the components (East/North) of v₂ - v₁.
Explore Question 3 of 10
Before running the simulation: set v₁ = 30 m/s (Car 1 North), v₂ = 20 m/s (Car 2 West). Predict the magnitude of the relative velocity of Car 2 as seen by Car 1. Show your reasoning and then verify with the simulation.
Explore Question 4 of 10
Keep v₁ = 30 m/s North and v₂ = 20 m/s. If you change Car 2 from West to East, how will the relative velocity vector change? Predict the new direction and magnitude before running the simulation. Verify with the simulation.
Explain Question 5 of 10
Calculate the velocity of Car 2 relative to Car 1 when Car 1 travels at 25 m/s North and Car 2 travels at 15 m/s West. Give your answer in component form (East, North), then find the magnitude and direction. Show all steps.
Explain Question 6 of 10
Derive the general expression for the magnitude of the relative velocity |v₂ - v₁| when Car 1 travels North at speed v₁ and Car 2 travels at speed v₂ at an angle θ measured counterclockwise from East. Express your answer in terms of v₁, v₂, and θ.
Elaborate Question 7 of 10
What happens to the relative velocity when both cars travel in the same direction (e.g., both North) at the same speed? Explain the physical meaning. Use the formula from Question 6 to support your answer.
Elaborate Question 8 of 10
If Car 2's speed approaches zero (it is nearly stationary at the intersection), what is v₂ relative to v₁? Describe what Car 1's driver observes. Use the relative velocity formula to explain.
