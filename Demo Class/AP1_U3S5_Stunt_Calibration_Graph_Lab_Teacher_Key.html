Rehearsal Drop Physical Explanation: At release height $h$, ball possesses initial GPE $U_{gi} = mgh$. As it rolls through loop apex ($y = 2r$), required centripetal acceleration is $a_c = v_{apex}^2/r$. If $v_{apex}^2 \ge gr$, track normal force $F_N \ge 0$, smoothly maintaining contact.

Energy Check & Dissipation Fee (Section 4 Analysis):
• Ideal theoretical point-mass slope: $2g = 2(9.80) = \mathbf{19.60\text{ m/s}^2}$ (100% translational mechanical efficiency).
• Measured apparatus slope: $a = \mathbf{8.627\text{ m/s}^2}$.
• Translational Energy Retained: $\eta = \frac{8.627}{19.60} \times 100\% = \mathbf{44.0\%}$.
• Mechanical Deficit / Dissipation Fee: $100\% - 44.0\% = \mathbf{56.0\%}$.
Physical Explanation: $\approx 56.0\%$ of initial gravitational potential energy is converted into rotational kinetic energy of the rolling solid sphere ($I = \frac{2}{5}mr^2$), rolling friction against rails, and micro-vibrations.

5. Target Range Inversion & Theoretical Formula Contrast

To demonstrate why empirical calibration is required, two test ranges below the maximum apparatus range ($X_{max} = 88.8\text{ cm}$) are solved below:

🎯 SAMPLE TARGET A: Contracted Landing Range $X^* = 78.0\text{ cm}$ (0.780 m)
1. Required Exit Speed: $(v^*)^2 = \frac{g (X^*)^2}{2H} = \frac{9.80(0.780)^2}{2(0.773)} = \mathbf{3.8566\text{ m}^2\text{/s}^2}$  ($v^* = 1.964\text{ m/s}$).
2. Empirical Calibration Inversion (True Setting):
$$h^* = \frac{(v^*)^2 - b}{a} = \frac{3.8566 - (-0.0740)}{8.6265} = \mathbf{45.56\text{ cm}} \; (0.4556\text{ m})$$ 3. Theoretical Formula Comparison (What naive theory predicts):
• Ideal Sliding Point-Mass ($mgh = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$): $h_{ideal}^* = \frac{(v^*)^2}{2g} = \mathbf{19.68\text{ cm}} \; (0.1968\text{ m})$.
• Pure Rolling Solid Sphere ($mgh = \frac{7}{10}mv^2$): $h_{rolling}^* = \frac{7(v^*)^2}{10g} = \mathbf{27.55\text{ cm}} \; (0.2755\text{ m})$.
DISCREPANCY ALERT: Ideal theory underpredicts required height by 56.8%! Releasing at $19.7\text{ cm}$ would fail to clear the $18.4\text{ cm}$ loop!

🎯 SAMPLE TARGET B: Contracted Landing Range $X^* = 85.0\text{ cm}$ (0.850 m)
1. Required Exit Speed: $(v^*)^2 = \frac{g (X^*)^2}{2H} = \frac{9.80(0.850)^2}{2(0.773)} = \mathbf{4.5799\text{ m}^2\text{/s}^2}$  ($v^* = 2.140\text{ m/s}$).
2. Empirical Calibration Inversion (True Setting):
$$h^* = \frac{(v^*)^2 - b}{a} = \frac{4.5799 - (-0.0740)}{8.6265} = \mathbf{53.95\text{ cm}} \; (0.5395\text{ m})$$ • Safe Limit Verification: $h^* = 53.95\text{ cm} < h_{max} = 58.3\text{ cm}$ (🟢 SAFE & VALID ON APPARATUS).
3. Theoretical Formula Underprediction: Ideal sliding $h_{ideal}^* = \mathbf{23.37\text{ cm}}$ (56.7% underprediction) | Pure rolling $h_{roll}^* = \mathbf{32.71\text{ cm}}$ (39.4% underprediction).

6. Student Exit Reflection Solutions & Official Scoring Rubric

Q1. Why use the calibrated line ($v^2 = ah + b$) rather than ideal physics?
Acceptable Answer: Real apparatus experiences mechanical energy loss to rotational inertia ($I = \frac{2}{5}mr^2$) and track friction. The empirical fit accounts directly for these real-world losses without arbitrary fudge factors.

Q2. What does the 56.0% slope deficit prove about mechanical energy?
Acceptable Answer: The slope deficit proves translational kinetic energy at exit is ~56.0% less than gravitational potential energy at release, proving non-conservative energy transfer away from linear motion.
