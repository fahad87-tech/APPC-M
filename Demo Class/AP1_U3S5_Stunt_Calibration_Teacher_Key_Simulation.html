3. Qualitative Physics Pre-Check (Section 1 Solution)

Free-Body Diagram at Loop Apex: Exactly two downward vectors directed toward loop centre: Gravitational force Fg = mg (downward) and Track Normal Force FN (downward). Common Student Error: Drawing an outward "centrifugal force" or an upward normal force. Zero credit for non-existent forces.

Rehearsal Drop Physical Explanation: At release height h, the ball possesses gravitational potential energy Ugi = mgh. At the apex (y = 2r), required centripetal acceleration is ac = vapex²/r. If vapex² ≥ gr, normal force FN ≥ 0, cleanly maintaining track contact.

4. Millimeter Regression Graph: v² vs. h (5 Data Points Plotted)

Vertical Axis: Exit speed squared v² (m²/s²) • Horizontal Axis: True release height h above exit datum (m).

Empirical Best-Fit Linear Equation (5 Points):

v² = (14.3995) h + (-0.1807)    (R² = 0.99966)
• Slope (a): 14.399 m/s²  |  • Y-Intercept (b): -0.181 m²/s²
Y-Intercept Interpretation: The small negative intercept (b = -0.181 m²/s²) physically reflects loop entry friction and rolling resistance threshold required before net horizontal exit velocity emerges.

Energy Check & Dissipation Fee (Section 4 Analysis):
• Ideal theoretical point-mass slope: 2g = 2(9.80) = 19.60 m/s² (100% translational mechanical efficiency).
• Measured apparatus slope: a = 14.399 m/s².
• Translational Energy Retained: η = (14.399 / 19.60) × 100% = 73.5%.
• Mechanical Deficit / Dissipation Fee: 100% − 73.5% = 26.5%.
Physical Explanation: ≈ 26.5% of initial gravitational potential energy is converted into rotational kinetic energy of the rolling solid sphere (I = ⅖mr²), rolling friction against rails, and micro-vibrations.

5. Target Range Inversion & Theoretical Formula Contrast

To demonstrate why empirical calibration is required, two test ranges below the maximum apparatus range (Xmax = 102.0 cm) are solved below:

🎯 SAMPLE TARGET A: Contracted Landing Range X* = 90.0 cm (0.900 m)
1. Required Exit Speed Squared:
(v*)² = g(X*)² / (2H) = 9.80(0.900)² / [2(0.773)] = 5.1345 m²/s²  (v* = 2.266 m/s).
2. Empirical Calibration Inversion (True Setting):

h* = [(v*)² − b] / a = [5.1345 − (-0.1807)] / 14.3995 = 36.91 cm (0.3691 m)
3. Theoretical Formula Comparison (What naive theory predicts):
• Ideal Sliding Point-Mass (mgh = ½mv²): hideal* = (v*)² / (2g) = 5.1345 / 19.60 = 26.20 cm (0.2620 m).
• Pure Rolling Solid Sphere (mgh = ⁷⁄₁₀mv²): hrolling* = 7(v*)² / (10g) = 36.68 cm (0.3668 m).
DISCREPANCY ALERT: Ideal sliding theory underpredicts required height by 29.0% (pure rolling underpredicts by 0.6%)! Releasing at 26.2 cm is below 2.5r (23.0 cm) and would fall off at the loop apex!

🎯 SAMPLE TARGET B: Contracted Landing Range X* = 86.0 cm (0.860 m)
1. Required Exit Speed Squared:
(v*)² = g(X*)² / (2H) = 9.80(0.860)² / [2(0.773)] = 4.6883 m²/s²  (v* = 2.165 m/s).
2. Empirical Calibration Inversion (True Setting):

h* = [(v*)² − b] / a = [4.6883 − (-0.1807)] / 14.3995 = 33.81 cm (0.3381 m)
• Safe Limit Verification: h* = 33.81 cm < hmax = 46.8 cm and ≥ 2.5r (23.0 cm) (🟢 SAFE & VALID ON APPARATUS).
3. Theoretical Formula Underprediction: Ideal sliding hideal* = 23.92 cm (29.3% deficit) | Pure rolling hrolling* = 33.49 cm (1.0% deficit).

6. Student Exit Reflection Solutions & Official Scoring Rubric

Q1. Why use the calibrated line (v² = ah + b) rather than ideal physics?
Acceptable Answer: Real apparatus experiences mechanical energy loss to rotational inertia (I = ⅖mr²) and track friction. The empirical fit accounts directly for these real-world losses without arbitrary fudge factors.

Q2. What does the 26.5% slope deficit prove about mechanical energy?
Acceptable Answer: The slope deficit proves translational kinetic energy at exit is ~26.5% less than gravitational potential energy at release, proving non-conservative energy transfer away from linear motion.
