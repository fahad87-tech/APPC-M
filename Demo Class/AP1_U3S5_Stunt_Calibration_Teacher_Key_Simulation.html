3. Qualitative Physics Pre-Check (Section 1 Solution)

Free-Body Diagram at Loop Apex: Exactly two downward vectors directed toward loop centre: Gravitational force $\vec{F}_g = m\vec{g}$ (down) and Track Normal Force $\vec{F}_N$ (down). Common Student Error: Drawing an outward "centrifugal force" or an extra upward vector. Zero credit for non-existent forces.

Rehearsal Drop Physical Explanation: At release height $h$, ball possesses initial GPE $U_{gi} = mgh$. As it rolls through loop apex ($y = 2r$), required centripetal acceleration is $a_c = v_{apex}^2/r$. If $v_{apex}^2 \ge gr$, track normal force $F_N \ge 0$, smoothly maintaining contact.

Energy Check & Dissipation Fee (Section 4 Analysis):
• Ideal theoretical point-mass slope: $2g = 2(9.80) = \mathbf{19.60\text{ m/s}^2}$ (100% translational mechanical efficiency).
• Measured apparatus slope: $a = \mathbf{13.144\text{ m/s}^2}$.
• Translational Energy Retained: $\eta = \frac{13.144}{19.60} \times 100\% = \mathbf{67.1\%}$.
• Mechanical Deficit / Dissipation Fee: $100\% - 67.1\% = \mathbf{32.9\%}$.
Physical Explanation: $\approx 32.9\%$ of initial gravitational potential energy is converted into rotational kinetic energy of the rolling solid sphere ($I = \frac{2}{5}mr^2$), rolling friction against rails, and micro-vibrations.

5. Target Range Inversion & Theoretical Formula Contrast

To demonstrate why empirical calibration is required, two test ranges below the maximum apparatus range ($X_{max} = 100.0\text{ cm}$) are solved below:

🎯 SAMPLE TARGET A: Contracted Landing Range $X^* = 90.0\text{ cm}$ (0.900 m)
1. Required Exit Speed: $(v^*)^2 = \frac{g (X^*)^2}{2H} = \frac{9.80(0.900)^2}{2(0.773)} = \mathbf{5.1345\text{ m}^2\text{/s}^2}$  ($v^* = 2.266\text{ m/s}$).
2. Empirical Calibration Inversion (True Setting):
$$h^* = \frac{(v^*)^2 - b}{a} = \frac{5.1345 - (+0.1943)}{13.1437} = \mathbf{37.59\text{ cm}} \; (0.3759\text{ m})$$ 3. Theoretical Formula Comparison (What naive theory predicts):
• Ideal Sliding Point-Mass ($mgh = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$): $h_{ideal}^* = \frac{(v^*)^2}{2g} = \mathbf{26.20\text{ cm}} \; (0.2620\text{ m})$.
• Pure Rolling Solid Sphere ($mgh = \frac{7}{10}mv^2$): $h_{rolling}^* = \frac{7(v^*)^2}{10g} = \mathbf{36.68\text{ cm}} \; (0.3668\text{ m})$.
DISCREPANCY ALERT: Ideal theory underpredicts required height by 30.3%! Releasing at $19.7\text{ cm}$ would fail to clear the $18.4\text{ cm}$ loop!

🎯 SAMPLE TARGET B: Contracted Landing Range $X^* = 86.0\text{ cm}$ (0.860 m)

6. Student Exit Reflection Solutions & Official Scoring Rubric

Q1. Why use the calibrated line ($v^2 = ah + b$) rather than ideal physics?
Acceptable Answer: Real apparatus experiences mechanical energy loss to rotational inertia ($I = \frac{2}{5}mr^2$) and track friction. The empirical fit accounts directly for these real-world losses without arbitrary fudge factors.

Q2. What does the 32.9% slope deficit prove about mechanical energy?
Acceptable Answer: The slope deficit proves translational kinetic energy at exit is ~32.9% less than gravitational potential energy at release, proving non-conservative energy transfer away from linear motion.
